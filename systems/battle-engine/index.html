Battle engine

Bank $C1 is the battle engine; nearly all of it is one system. The battle runs with DP=$0000 and DB=$7E, and keeps its state in bank-$7E WRAM (names in asm/include/ram_battle.inc, all starting with Battle, Battler, Pc, Enemy, Tech, Item or Sfx). Long helpers in bank $FD (BattleFD_*) do much of its setup and its ends.

This page covers the battle's structure and the shared services. The parts with their own pages: Battle actions (service 4 and the action scripts), Battle menu, Battle targeting and Status bar & text.

"Victory", "defeat", "attack", "tech", "item" and the like below follow the headers, which take them from the services' and records' known roles; where a header hedges, so does this page.

Entering and leaving

The field enters the battle with JSL $C10000 (in DefaultHandler, $C0:18A7). Per the headers that chain runs JMP $001B → JML $CF:FB65 (which clears the battle RAM) → $C1:8000 (BankC1_BattleStartVec) → BattleSys_Main. The battle leaves through BattleSys_ExitVec ($C1:0006 → $001F → JML $CF:FBE5); the vectors at $C1:0000–$0050 and the code in bank $CF are not matched yet. On the field side Scene_Unk0283 reads FieldBtl_Result to decide how to come back.

BattleSys_Main

BattleSys_Main ($C1:8006, 1,115 bytes) is the battle from its setup to its end; it never returns.

Setup. BattleFD_UnkA982, the random index, Battle_SetupBattle ($C1:FA8B: the battlers' records, the enemies' stats, the PCs' tech menus, the turn-list state, mostly through bank-$FD callees), then service 0. Then two shuffles with Battle_RandRange: Battle_SlotOrder, a random order of the battlers taking part, and Battle_EnemyOrder, the present enemies in a random order. Then BattleSys_Unk8C09, which runs the main part of each enemy's behaviour script once.

Each pass of the main loop: service 3 (BattleSys_UpkeepTwoFrames, two frames of upkeep), the KO checks (BattleSys_UnkB223), then the first of:

  • Battle_Unk24 set: the defeat end;
  • no enemy entry left: the victory end;
  • otherwise the low-HP bits and the turn-list membership of every battler (BattleSys_UnkB093, BattleSys_UnkB0B6), then by state: in wait mode (Battle_MenuTimeHold) only the PCs' pending commands; with Battle_Unk99CD set its end (probably running away; not traced); with Battle_UnkAF25 set the turn handler BattleSys_Unk8461 directly; else the turn lists.

Ends. Victory plays service 8 (BattleSys_VictoryPose), puts the max HP back and adds the gold and item rewards; defeat runs service 3 eight times and then service 9 (BattleSys_DefeatPose). A dead debug check (.debug_check, pad 2) can end the battle as a win. All ends save the random index back and copy Battle_Unk1C48 entries to the PCs' BattlerStats.Unk56 before BattleFD_UnkAAB0.

Turn lists

Thirteen per-slot lists ($C1:8461–$C1:8C3D), each one byte per list and battler slot in four 13 × 11-byte arrays: Battle_ListTimers (countdowns), Battle_ListFlags (bit 7 due, low bits "takes part"), Battle_ListRuns (runs left) and Battle_ListReload (timer reload). BattleSys_Main counts the timers down each pass; every second pass it scans each list for the next due slot in Battle_SlotOrder and runs that list's entry of BattleSys_ListHandlerTable ($C1:B92D).

List Handler What it does
0, 2–5, 10 BattleSys_ListHandler0 … The usual handler: re-arm the entry, count its runs down, and at 0 clear one status bit of the slot and stop the entry
1 BattleSys_ListHandler1 A repeating hit on the slot (inferred from where the amount goes)
6, 7, 11 BattleSys_ListHandler6/7/11 Nothing (RTS)
8, 9 BattleSys_ListHandler8/9 Re-arm with the timer halved or doubled by status bits
12 BattleSys_Unk8461 The battlers' turns: a ready PC is queued for the command menu (service 1), a ready enemy acts (BattleAi_EnemyTurn), committed PC commands run (BattleSys_UnkB967), then the enemies' reaction scripts

The banner reads lists 0–5 and 8–10 as timed statuses from their shape; which status each list is, is not traced.

Enemy behaviour scripts

An enemy's script lives in bank $CC at the address !BattleRom_AiScriptPtrs gives for its id. It has two parts, each ended by $FF: the main part, run for each enemy by BattleSys_Unk8C09 / BattleAi_RunMainPart ($C1:AFD2), and the reaction part, run by BattleSys_Unk8461 after a turn (BattleAi_FindReactPart, $C1:B488). A part is a list of blocks; a block is one or two 4-byte test records ending in $FE, then action records ending in $FE. The first byte of a record is its code:

Table Address Entries Run by
BattleAi_TestTable $C1:B80D 41 test codes $00–$28 BattleAi_RunMainPart, the reaction loop
BattleAi_ChooseTable $C1:B85F 23 action codes $00–$16 BattleAi_TestPassed, as a block is chosen
BattleAi_RunTable $C1:B88D the same 23 action codes BattleAi_EnemyTurn, on the enemy's turn
BattleAi_TargetTable $C1:B8BB 57 target codes $00–$38 BattleAi_ReadTargets

A test that holds calls BattleAi_TestPassed ($C1:8C3E), which runs the block's next action through the choose table; on the enemy's turn BattleAi_EnemyTurn ($C1:8CF9) runs it again through the run table; the action block that service 4 plays is started by BattleAi_FillActBlock. Target codes fill the list BattleAi_Targets, with helpers that pick PCs by a stat-block byte, cut the list down to one, or keep the marked entries ($C1:AC14–$C1:AF21). The names "test", "action" and "target" are inferred from what the matched handlers do. The handlers themselves (BattleAi_TestNN etc., $C1:8EAB–$C1:AC13) are not matched yet, except test $00, which always holds.

PC commands and techs

BattleSys_UnkB967 ($C1:B967, the "PC command runner") runs the command a PC committed in the battle menu, from its BattleCmd record: an attack through BattleSys_RunPcAttack, an item as action kind 3, or a tech as kind 2 with its users' MP paid (BattleSys_PayTechMp); then it reloads the users' gauges (!Battler_UnkAFAB, probably the ATB gauge's reload value) and clears the records (BattleSys_UnkBCE1, BattleSys_ResetTechUsers).

Battle_SetupBattle builds each PC's 20 tech menu entries with BattleSys_UnkC96A, BattleSys_UnkCA1A and BattleSys_UnkCCCB ($C1:C96A–$C1:CDFE): single techs at entries 0–7, up to 6 double techs at 10–15 and a triple tech at 18, each only when all its users are in the party. A tech's record names its users and each user's part; BattleSys_TechMpCost1-3 read each part's MP cost and BattleSys_AdjustMpCost halves or quarters it by a per-PC byte.

Before a command runs, BattleSys_UnkC1DD ($C1:C1DD–$C1:C8F6) checks that the caster and its partners can act (not KO'd, no status that stops them) and resolves the targets into a list of slots from the tech's, item's or enemy action's target mode and area number; if that fails the action is dropped.

Effects and damage

A tech (or a PC's attack) runs as up to three parts, one per user. BattleSys_RunTechParts ($C1:D523) checks the MP, then for each part copies a 12-byte effect record whose byte 0, the effect type, picks a routine from BattleSys_EffectTable ($C1:D9D7, 45 words; types $09–$28 do nothing). An item runs one of types $29–$2B straight from BattleSys_UnkBF46, and the enemy runners BattleSys_UnkD7C4 / D8D1 use the same table. The effect routines ($C1:D221–$C1:D48E) work on the targets from the last to the first. Their headers read them, "probably", as: 0 restores HP / MP, 1 cures statuses, 2 sets statuses, 3 is a hit with damage, 5 drains HP / MP, 6 steals, 7 gives up the caster's HP, 8 repeats type 3.

The arithmetic they call ($C1:DA37–$C1:E89E, and BattleSys_StealItem at $C1:E9D5):

  • Restore (BattleSys_RestoreHpMp): HP by an amount × a factor plus a random part (for a PC capped at 999), or the full maximum for factor $0F; MP the same way, in tens.
  • Statuses: BattleSys_CureStatus clears them (KO too, with a flag) and the PC's turn-list flags; BattleSys_SetStatus sets status bits in one of six status bytes (for the first three, only when the target lacks those bits in a byte the header reads as "probably the target's protection"), and BattleSys_ArmStatusList starts the turn list that times the new status (lists 0, 3, 4 and 5 clear their bit when they run out).
  • Type 3, a hit: an enemy-only bit test ("probably an immunity"), then the hit roll (BattleSys_RollHit: chance = caster's value × 100 / target's value against a roll of 0–99, the two values first adjusted by statuses in BattleSys_AdjustHit / BattleSys_AdjustEvade), then the damage. BattleSys_CalcDamage picks a formula by an effect byte (kinds 1–7), each a mix of the caster's stats scaled by another effect byte, with a random part bounded by one of the fixed fractions (Battle_Scale*, $C1:C02A–$C1:C1DC: about x / 5, x × 3 / 10 and so on, built from shifts and subtractions); the caster's status bits then raise or cut the amount. BattleSys_AdjustDefence gives a defence out of 256 from an effect byte or the target's stats, cut by its statuses, and BattleSys_ApplyDamage takes (256 − defence) / 256 of the damage plus the random part, at least 1, cut to 7/10 by two statuses (probably barriers) unless the effect pierces them. A hit also clears two status bits that BattleSys_WakeTarget reads as a status a hit ends ("sleep, probably").
  • Drain (BattleSys_DrainHpMp, type 5): a share of the target's maximum HP or MP plus a random part, capped at what it has, taken from the target and given to the caster.
  • Steal (BattleSys_StealItem, type 6, or type 3 with an effect bit): "an item taken from the target enemy, probably". A roll of 0–99 against a chance from the effect record (tripled for one caster value); on success one of the enemy's two reward items goes to the caster's item record or the inventory. Each of the two can be taken once.

Damage and stealing also run handlers from BattleSys_UnkB2CCHandlerTable ($C1:FA61, 21 words), picked by byte 0 of a 3-byte record (BattleSys_RunUnkB2CCHandlers, $C1:EA6F; BattleSys_RunUnkB2CCHandler, $C1:EB1C). The handlers themselves ($C1:ED89 on) are not matched, and what they do is not analysed.

The headers keep the quirks found here, among them BattleSys_CalcDamage's test for kind 8, which branches the wrong way so that its block runs for every kind but 1–8 and the code for kinds 9 and 10 is never reached.

Hit records

Amounts to take off HP or MP are 4-byte entries per battler slot, in sets of 11 ($C1:E89F–$C1:E8BF, $C1:EBF8–$C1:ED88): Battle_RecordHit stores one (negated, it heals), Battle_ClearCancelledHits drops the cancelled ones, and Battle_ApplyHits ($C1:EC7F) takes them off CurHp / CurMp, clamps to the maximum, and KOs a battler whose HP reaches 0 (an enemy adds its rewards first, BattleFD_AddEnemyRewards).

Battler upkeep

$C1:B093–$C1:B487: the low-HP bit (HP at most an eighth of the maximum), which turn lists a battler takes part in from its status bits, the KO check with a one-time revive (BattleSys_UpdateKo), and the copies back to the field at the end (FieldBtlObj flags, the character records). The turn helpers ($C1:B4E9–$C1:B80C) refund items, build the PCs' command groups from their partner bytes, and handle a battle with fixed commands (Battle_Unk2989 bit 5, "a demo, probably" in NEXT.md).

Bank $FD helpers

BattleFD_* ($FD:A982–$FD:B956, 40 routines) are the battle's long callees: clearing work RAM, restoring enemies, the end messages and rewards (BattleFD_AddEnemyRewards, BattleFD_AddItemEntry), the battlers' records, the enemies' stats, the turn-list timers and the first ATB values. They run in the battle's state and use bank $C1's direct-page scratch and math cells. Five JSR/RTL veneers at $C1:FDBF–$C1:FDD2 (Battle_Mul16Long, BankC1_AddItemLong, BattleSys_RunPcAttackLong, Battle_RandRangeLong, BattleSys_RefundItemLong) let them call back into bank $C1. Most keep Unk names because verified code calls them by name; NEXT.md lists better names for when those callers are reviewed again.

Shared services and helpers

  • Inventory and gold services for the field: BankC1_Entry8003 ($C1:8003) leads to BankC1_RunService ($C1:CFC2) and BankC1_ServiceTable ($C1:D126): 0 find an item, 1 add one, 2 remove one, 3 test the 24-bit gold against an amount, 4 add gold, 5 take gold off when there is that much. The event opcodes $C7, $C9–$CE use them (see Field event scripts).
  • The service dispatcher at $C1:0045 (table at $C1:0051, reached only by same-bank JSR): 1 enqueue a ready battler, 2 remove one, 3 two frames of upkeep, 4 play an action, 5 position queries (BattlePos_Query), 7 target area query, 8 victory pose, 9 defeat pose; service 0 ends in Battle_StartPose, service 6 is not analysed.
  • Frame wait: BattleSys_PumpFrames ($C1:007E), a frame wait by inference (see Frame cycle and NMI).
  • Maths: Battle_Mul8, Battle_Mul8x16 and Battle_Divide on the hardware maths unit ($C1:0089–$C1:010C), the overlapping shift tails ($C1:010D–$C1:011E), the shift-and-add Battle_Mul16 and Battle_Div32 ($C1:C90B–$C1:C95B), and Battle_RandRange ($C1:AF22), a random number in a range from RandomTableFD.
  • Geometry: Battle_SinLookup and Battle_CalcAngle ($C1:01F9–$C1:0298, 256 steps per turn); battler boxes and their collision tests ($C1:283D–$C1:2985); the position queries of service 5 ($C1:2986–$C1:2D9E: nearest or farthest PC or enemy, distance checks, a path check, screen-half tests).
  • Animation: the battler frame decoder (Battle_DrawBattlerFrameAnyLayout, $C1:1C55), the PC and enemy animation ticks, the status animation picker and its eight effect handlers ($C1:2F97–$C1:3233), the PC poses, and the enemy movers (see Battle actions).
